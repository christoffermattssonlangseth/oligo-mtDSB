### 7. Neural stem cells mount a proliferative and neurogenic response to oligodendrocyte-restricted mitochondrial stress
Although mitochondrial double-strand breaks (mtDSBs) were induced specifically in oligodendrocytes, we observed a striking **non–cell-autonomous response** in ventricular-zone neural stem cells (NSCs). At postnatal day 21 (P21), NSCs in the ventricular system showed only a modest and spatially confined activation signature, with upregulation of stress/adaptive and barrier-associated genes (e.g. *Cldn10*, *Gpc4*, *Tsc22d3*). This suggests that at early time points, the niche registers the disturbance but does not yet undergo a major state transition.

By postnatal day 60 (P60), however, ventricular NSCs displayed one of the **strongest regional activation indices** of any population in the dataset. The response at this stage was dominated by coordinated induction of canonical cell-cycle drivers and neurogenic competence factors — including *Ccna2*, *Mki67*, *Myt1*, *Dlx2*, *Dlx5*, *Arx*, *Stmn4*, *Sox1*, *Sox2*, *Sox3*, *Sox4*, *Dcx*, and *Tubb2b* — together with chromatin and transcriptional regulators associated with lineage priming and neuroblast output (*Gad1*, *Gad2*, *Foxg1*, *Lmx1b*, *Lef1*, *Cux2*, *Hist2h2bb*). Many of these genes exhibited large positive log₂ fold-changes (often >2) with extremely strong statistical support (adjusted p-values <1×10⁻¹⁰), consistent with a **global, niche-wide transcriptional shift** rather than sporadic activation in isolated cells.

Neural stem cells in the ventricular system thus showed a **qualitatively distinct response** compared to other cell classes. Whereas oligodendrocytes, astrocytes, and microglia mounted stress-related programs already at P21 ([See section on differential gene expression](3-agexcondition-glia.md#3-differential-gene-expression-reveals-cell-type–specific-stress-remodeling-and-immune–metabolic-adaptation)) and then intensified those programs by P60, NSCs exhibited little or no transcriptional change at P21. By P60, however, they underwent a dramatic induction of proliferative and neurogenic factors (e.g. *Ccna2*, *Mki67*, *Dlx1*, *Dlx2*, *Sox11*, *Dcx*, *Tubb3*, *Gad1*), alongside cytoskeletal and migratory effectors (*Stmn2*, *Stmn4*, *Dcx*). This shift suggests that chronic oligodendrocyte mitochondrial stress is eventually communicated to the periventricular niche, where it is sufficient to drive NSCs out of quiescence and into an activated, progenitor-like state — but only in the mature brain. In other words, **neural stem cells behave as late reporters of accumulated oligodendrocyte distress**, rather than as early responders.

Two features stand out. First, this program is **not a generic inflammatory response**; it resembles a *reactivation or pro-neurogenic* state, with proliferation markers (*Mki67*, *Ccna2*), proneurogenic transcription factors (*Dlx2*, *Dlx5*, *Arx*, *Foxg1*), neuronal lineage markers (*Dcx*, *Gad1*, *Gad2*), cytoskeletal and migratory regulators (*Stmn4*, *Tubb2b*), and synaptic or axonal remodeling genes (*Syp*, *Map1b*, *Plxna3*) all co-induced. Second, this occurs **despite NSCs not harboring mtDNA damage**. Instead, they appear to be responding to a chronically altered environment shaped by metabolically and transcriptionally reprogrammed oligodendrocytes and their associated glia.

We interpret this as evidence that the **ventricular stem cell niche senses and integrates long-range stress cues** emanating from mtDSB-bearing oligodendrocytes in distant white-matter tracts. By P60 — when oligodendrocytes have transitioned into a persistent ISR- and mTOR-inhibited, immune-alert state — NSCs react as though the tissue requires repair: they upregulate proliferation programs, neurogenic lineage factors, and early neuronal specification modules. In other words, **mitochondrial injury in oligodendrocytes is sufficient to broadcast a “damage/remodel” signal to the adult neural stem cell niche**, pushing the ventricular system toward a regenerative-like, pro-neurogenic state.

This finding highlights that **white-matter mitochondrial stress is not cell-autonomous**: it is sensed at the systems level, and one of the earliest extra-lineage responders is the ventricular stem cell compartment.
